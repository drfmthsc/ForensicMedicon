About the Conference

FORENSIC MEDICON 2027 is the 48th National Conference of the Indian Academy of Forensic Medicine, hosted by Swami Rama Himalayan University in Dehradun.

48th Edition 2–6 February 2027 Dehradun, Uttarakhand 5 Skill Labs 6 Disciplines

Who it brings together

Experts from across Forensic Medicine, Nursing, Forensic Science, Law and Criminal Justice.

This year's edition is organised with the active support of the Trained Nurses' Association of India (TNAI), Uttarakhand State Branch.

Why it matters

Justice depends on coordination. Police investigators, medical professionals, forensic scientists, the judiciary and society each hold one link of the same chain.

When any link is weak, evidence fails — however sound the science behind it.

The nursing frontline

Nurses are the first to meet victims of trauma and violence, often hours before an investigator arrives.

They bridge clinical care with early evidence preservation and meticulous medico-legal documentation — which is why nursing sits at the centre of this year's theme.

Translational
Forensics

Turning academic research, nursing insight and scientific innovation into three practical things: investigative tools that work in the field, bedside practice that preserves evidence, and courtroom evidence that holds up under scrutiny.

Philosophical Inspiration

Inspired by the vision of Swami Rama, founder of the institution, who emphasised the integration of science, spirituality and discipline in the pursuit of truth and service. FORENSIC MEDICON 2027 carries this philosophy into medico-legal practice, where science must be applied with responsibility, humility and in the service of justice.

Learn the path of selflessness, because that is the only way of liberation. There is no other way.

Conference Theme

Translational Forensics

Uniting Medicine, Nursing, Forensics, Judiciary, Police and Society

Forensic Medicine does not function in isolation.

Its impact is realised only when scientific findings pass intact through every hand that touches them.

Documented→Investigated→Analysed→Interpreted→Trusted

What the theme sets out to do

  • 01

    Coordination

    Strengthen coordination between police investigators, medical experts, nurses and forensic laboratories.

  • 02

    Documentation

    Improve the quality and reliability of medico-legal documentation and forensic reporting.

  • 03

    Legal understanding

    Enhance understanding of forensic evidence within the legal and judicial system.

  • 04

    Integrity

    Promote scientific integrity and ethical practice in forensic investigations.

  • 05

    Public trust

    Build public confidence in forensics as a tool for justice.

Flagship session

The Justice Chain Forum

An interdisciplinary dialogue bringing every institution involved in justice delivery into the same room — for once, at the same table.

What it examines

  • Systemic gaps between institutions
  • Practical challenges in day-to-day casework
  • Opportunities to improve coordination across the chain

What it produces

Not a panel discussion that ends when the session does. The forum sets out to deliver practical recommendations for strengthening the role of Forensic Medicine within the justice system.

Police investigatorsForensic medicine expertsForensic scientistsNursesPublic prosecutorsJudicial officersLegal scholars

Travel Planner

Dehradun is well connected by air, rail and road — and the venue sits beside Jolly Grant Airport, so most delegates are on campus within minutes of landing.

By air
Jolly Grant Airport (DED) serves Dehradun — and the venue sits right beside it. Direct flights from Delhi, Mumbai, Bengaluru and Lucknow.

Minutes from the venue · Delhi in about an hour

By rail
Dehradun Railway Station (DDN) has daytime and overnight services from Delhi, including the Shatabdi and Nanda Devi Express.

About 25 km to the venue

By road
Roughly six hours from Delhi on NH-7, with frequent buses from ISBT Kashmere Gate and taxis available throughout.

About 235 km from Delhi

Conference se seekhenge vigyan ki baatein, aur pahadon se seekhenge jeevan ki raahein

Conference week is winter. Expect roughly 6–22 °C in the valley, near-freezing nights at Dhanaulti and Chakrata, and a real chance of snow above 2000 m. Pack layers — and if the hills are why you're coming, February is the right week for it.
Distances below are measured from Dehradun city. The venue sits at Jolly Grant, about 25 km east on the Rishikesh road — so Rishikesh and Haridwar are closer than they look, and Mussoorie and Chakrata a little further.

Close by

An evening, or a couple of hours between sessions
Forest Research Institute7 km · Dehradun

A 1906 Greco-Roman colonial building set in a 450-hectare estate, with six museums of forestry and botany inside. The most photographed building in the city.

  • Six museums under one roof
  • Vast lawns and sal forest
  • Allow about two hours
Mindrolling Monastery10 km · Clement Town

One of the largest Tibetan Buddhist stupas in the world, in a quiet monastery complex on the south-west edge of the city.

  • The Great Stupa
  • Buddha statue and gardens
Robber's Cave8–9 km · Guchhupani

A natural river cave where water flows through narrow rock walls. Visitors walk barefoot through the cool stream.

  • Best time: morning to afternoon
  • Popular for photography and picnics
Sahastradhara15 km

Famous for its thousand-fold spring waterfalls and limestone formations. The sulphur-rich water is believed to have therapeutic properties.

  • Ropeway, swimming pools and scenic views
Tapkeshwar Temple6 km

A cave temple on the Tons river where water drips continuously onto the Shivling. Busiest on Mondays and at Shivratri.

  • Natural cave shrine
  • Stream running through the complex
Paltan Bazaar & Clock TowerCity centre

Dehradun's oldest market street, running from the Clock Tower. The place to pick up rusks, woollens and Tibetan handicrafts.

  • Bakeries and street food
  • Open into the evening
Malsi Deer Park10 km · Mussoorie road

A small zoological park at the foot of the hills, with deer, peacocks and a children's area. The easiest option for delegates travelling with family.

  • Good for accompanying persons
  • Under an hour is enough

Half a day

Comfortable either side of a session day
Mussoorie35 km · Queen of Hills

The most famous hill station near Dehradun, and the classic half-day escape.

  • Mall Road
  • Kempty Falls
  • Gun Hill viewpoint
  • Sunset views and cafés
Landour40 km · above Mussoorie

A colonial cantonment on the ridge above Mussoorie — quieter and steeper than the town below. Deodar, tin roofs and a walk that loops the hilltop.

  • Char Dukan — four cafés at the top of the climb
  • Lal Tibba, the highest point
  • Sisters Bazaar and Prakash’s Store
  • Cambridge Book Depot, long associated with Ruskin Bond
Rishikesh45 km from Dehradun · closer to the venue

Yoga capital of the world, on the Ganga where it leaves the hills. Jolly Grant sits between the city and Rishikesh, so this is nearer than the city-centre distance suggests.

  • Ganga Aarti at Parmarth Niketan
  • Ram Jhula and Lakshman Jhula
  • The Beatles Ashram
  • Rafting at Shivpuri — in season in February
Haridwar45 km

One of the holiest cities in India and a major pilgrimage destination. Lamps go on the water at dusk — arrive an hour early for a place on the steps.

  • Ganga Aarti at Har Ki Pauri

Food Trail of Dehradun

Dehradun is known not only for its scenic surroundings and academic institutions, but for a rich and diverse culinary culture — from legendary bakeries to Tibetan delicacies and Garhwali traditional dishes.

Legendary bakeries

Famous across India for crisp rusks, cakes and old-world charm — perfect for quick bites or souvenirs to take home.

  • Butter rusks
  • Plum cake
  • Stick jaw toffee
  • Chocolate pastries

Ellora's Melting Moments · Sunrise Bakery

Tibetan & Himalayan

Dehradun's proximity to Tibetan settlements has enriched the city's food culture.

  • Steamed or fried momos
  • Thukpa — Tibetan noodle soup
  • Tingmo with gravy
  • Laphing — a street snack

Kalsang Friends Corner

Authentic Garhwali

Traditional Himalayan heritage on a plate. Garhwali cuisine is not just food — it is nutrition shaped by the mountains.

  • Kafuli — spinach-based dish
  • Chainsoo — roasted black gram curry
  • Mandua (finger millet) roti
  • Jhangora kheer — millet dessert
Pahadon wali Maggi

Almost a culture in the Himalayan hill stations. Cooked at small roadside tapris, eaten with cutting chai and a mountain view.

  • Mussoorie Mall Road stalls
  • Lal Tibba viewpoint cafés
  • The Mussoorie–Dhanaulti road

Many travellers insist it tastes better above 2000 m

Mandua momos

A local innovation: the dumpling wrapper is made with mandua (finger millet / ragi) instead of refined flour — higher in fibre and calcium, slightly earthy and nutty.

  • Filled with vegetables, paneer or chicken
  • Found in Dehradun, Mussoorie and Rishikesh

Part of the growing "Pahadi fusion cuisine" movement
